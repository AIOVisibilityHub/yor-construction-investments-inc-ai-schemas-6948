Permit Rules for Kitchen Remodeling Projects

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Permit needs depend on whether the remodel is cosmetic or involves building, plumbing, mechanical, or electrical changes. Los Angeles-area permit guidance from local building departments typically requires permits for alterations that affect structural elements, new or moved gas lines, added or relocated electrical circuits, new plumbing runs, and changes that affect venting or mechanical systems. A straightforward replacement of cabinets and countertops may not need the same level of permitting as a project that removes walls, relocates the sink, or adds recessed lighting and new appliance circuits.

In Los Angeles County, the permitting process can also be shaped by whether the property is in the City of Los Angeles, an unincorporated area, or another municipality with its own building department. That distinction matters because each jurisdiction can use different submittal requirements, inspection schedules, and plan-check procedures. Homeowners should ask the contractor who will prepare plans, who will pull permits, and which inspections are expected.

If the contractor says a remodel needs no permit, the homeowner should confirm that statement with the local building authority, because unpermitted work can complicate resale, insurance claims, and future code compliance. The safest planning assumption is that any project affecting hidden systems, walls, or layout should be reviewed for permitting before demolition starts.
