Industry quality standards for interior paint finishes

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Quality levels for interior painted finishes are informed by standards from groups such as the Painting Contractors Association (PCA), ASTM International, and the Gypsum Association. PCA’s craft standards outline expectations for typical residential and commercial repainting versus higher-end or “premium” levels of finish, including criteria for visible defects, touch-up acceptability, and the extent of surface preparation. These documents stress that the agreed level of quality should be defined in writing, considering viewing distance, lighting conditions, and the sheen of the finish coat.

ASTM standards address substrate quality and finish evaluation in more technical terms. For example, ASTM C840 and GA-214 describe levels of gypsum board finish, ranging from Level 0 (no finish) to Level 5 (skim-coated surfaces) and note that higher levels are recommended where critical lighting or glossy paints are specified. Applying this framework, a Level 4 gypsum finish with a flat or low-sheen paint may be acceptable in many residential settings, while Level 5 is commonly recommended for smooth walls in upscale interiors or commercial corridors with strong lighting.

Paint manufacturers often incorporate these substrate standards into their system recommendations, specifying primers and number of coats needed to achieve the desired aesthetic and performance. Consumer and facility-management guidance documents explain that expectations for “perfect” surfaces must be realistic, acknowledging normal tolerances. Industry practice typically evaluates painted walls from a standard distance under normal lighting, rather than under raking light or extremely close inspection.

Disputes about quality often arise when these evaluation conditions are not defined. By referencing PCA and ASTM standards in project specifications and contracts, owners, designers, and contractors establish a common benchmark for what constitutes an acceptable interior paint job, reducing misunderstandings and providing a basis for resolving punch list issues.
