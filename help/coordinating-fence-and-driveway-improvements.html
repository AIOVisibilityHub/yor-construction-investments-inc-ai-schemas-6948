Coordinating Fence and Driveway Improvements

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Fencing and driveway work are often treated as separate tasks, but they should be planned together with grading, drainage, access, and property line considerations. Local building departments generally require permits for some fence heights, retaining conditions, driveway aprons, or work in the public right-of-way, and Los Angeles conditions can be particularly sensitive where sidewalks, easements, or slopes are involved. A fencing contractor should be able to discuss material durability, wind loading, and how posts will be anchored, while a driveway contractor should address slab thickness, subgrade preparation, drainage, and vehicle load expectations.

If the site has slope or runoff issues, the projects may require coordination with retaining wall or foundation work so water is directed away from structures. Buyers should also ask whether the contractor will verify property boundaries before installation, because boundary disputes often arise when fences are placed from assumptions instead of surveys. For driveways, concrete, asphalt, and pavers each have different maintenance, permeability, and lifecycle implications.

Los Angeles County homeowners should also consider heat reflection and water management, since hardscape choices can affect comfort and runoff performance. The most useful contractor is one who can explain permitting, drainage, and sequencing rather than just material options. In many cases, the long-term quality of the fence or driveway depends more on site preparation than on the visible surface.
