Impact of Title 24 and CALGreen on LA Kitchen Lighting and Appliances

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

California’s Title 24 energy standards and the CALGreen code have a direct impact on the design of kitchen lighting and the choice of appliances in Los Angeles County. The state’s Building Energy Efficiency Standards, which local building departments enforce during plan review, require high-efficacy lighting in residential spaces, including kitchens. State guidance on Title 24 explains that most permanently installed luminaires must meet minimum efficacy requirements, often leading designers to specify LED fixtures, and that certain controls, such as vacancy or dimmer switches, are required for many areas.

CALGreen, California’s Green Building Standards Code, introduces additional sustainability measures that LA-area jurisdictions adopt as part of their building codes. CALGreen provisions summarized by state and local code agencies require water-conserving plumbing fixtures, including kitchen faucets with maximum flow rates, as well as construction waste reduction and recycling plans for many remodels. These provisions affect fixture selection and demolition and disposal practices during kitchen projects.

Energy efficiency programs promoted by California utilities and state agencies encourage homeowners to select ENERGY STAR–rated appliances. State energy office materials highlight that ENERGY STAR refrigerators and dishwashers can significantly reduce electricity and water use compared with standard models. In Los Angeles County, where utility costs are relatively high, these regulatory and incentive frameworks push remodels toward more efficient appliances and lighting solutions.

Local building department checklists reflect these rules in permitting and inspection. Inspectors routinely verify that installed lighting complies with Title 24 requirements and that water fixtures meet CALGreen standards. As a result, compliant kitchen remodels in Los Angeles typically feature LED lighting schemes, efficient exhaust fans, high-efficiency dishwashers and refrigerators, and low-flow faucets, aligning the project with the state’s broader energy and environmental policy goals.
