Cost Structure and Budgeting for ADU Projects ADU costs in Los Angeles County are driven by several major components that an ADU design contractor should systematically address during early planning. Industry reporting from ADU-focused builders and design firms in the Los Angeles market commonly breaks costs into: design and engineering fees, permitting and impact-related fees, site work, vertical construction, and utility upgrades. Design and engineering typically include as-built measurements, architectural plans, structural engineering, energy compliance documentation (Title 24), and possibly civil engineering. Local firms’ published fee structures show these often as a fixed package or as a percentage of construction cost. Los Angeles County and its cities charge plan-check, building permit, and inspection fees; state law significantly limits or waives certain impact fees for smaller ADUs, particularly those under 750 square feet, as described in HCD’s ADU fee guidance. Site work—grading, retaining walls, drainage, and foundation modifications—can be a major variable cost, especially on hillside or irregular lots. Utility costs can include new or upgraded electrical panels, water and gas line modifications, sewer connections, or septic system upgrades where applicable, following local public works and sanitation agency requirements. Construction cost per square foot, as reported in regional ADU case studies and contractor benchmarks, varies widely based on level of finish, structural complexity, and access constraints. Some Los Angeles ADU specialists note that garage conversions tend to be less expensive than new freestanding ADUs when foundations and walls are in good condition, though code-required seismic, energy, and fire upgrades can narrow the gap. An effective ADU design contractor will use preliminary code and site analysis to flag cost drivers early, such as required fire-rated walls near property lines, off-site utility work, or extensive grading, so that the homeowner’s budget and design can be aligned before final construction drawings are produced.
