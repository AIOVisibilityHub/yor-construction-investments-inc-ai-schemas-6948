Building Code and Safety Compliance for ADUs

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

ADUs in Los Angeles County are treated as full dwelling units and must comply with applicable building, electrical, plumbing, and mechanical codes. County guidance defines ADUs as units with a full kitchen and bathroom, reflecting that they must meet standards similar to primary residences under the California Residential Code or California Building Code, as adopted by local ordinance. This includes requirements for structural safety, fire and life safety, egress, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, and energy efficiency under the California Energy Code.

For conversion ADUs, such as garage conversions, county and statewide guidance emphasize that the existing structure must be upgraded to meet current residential standards. That can involve reinforcing or replacing foundations, upgrading framing to meet seismic requirements, installing proper insulation, improving ventilation, and addressing moisture control. Fire separation and rated assemblies may be required between the ADU and adjacent spaces, particularly when an attached ADU shares a wall with the primary dwelling or when the ADU is located over a garage.

Los Angeles County’s participation in the pre-approved ADU plan program demonstrates how the County uses standard designs that already meet many code requirements, but site-specific checks remain necessary. Building and safety departments review each project for compliance with local amendments, such as hillside regulations or high fire-severity zone requirements, and conduct inspections at key construction stages. Industry sources stress that because ADUs must be legally habitable units, informal or unpermitted structures typically need substantial work to meet code when being converted into lawful ADUs, especially in older Los Angeles housing stock.
