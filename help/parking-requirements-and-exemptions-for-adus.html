Parking Requirements and Exemptions for ADUs

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

California’s state ADU laws significantly restrict local governments’ ability to impose parking requirements that would make ADUs infeasible. Statewide guidance for 2025, reflected in ADU explainer materials, notes that jurisdictions cannot require parking for ADUs in many common situations, such as when the property is within a certain distance of public transit, is located in a historic district, or involves the conversion of existing structures like garages. These rules are designed to encourage ADU production, especially in urban and transit-accessible areas.

Los Angeles County implements these state mandates through its local ADU ordinance and planning practices. County-level materials emphasize that ADUs are accessory to existing residential uses and generally do not trigger the same parking ratios as new primary dwellings. For garage conversion ADUs, state law permits jurisdictions to forgo replacement parking in many scenarios, and policy summaries for Southern California highlight this as a key driver of garage conversion feasibility in Los Angeles neighborhoods where on-street parking is common but off-street space is limited.

Planning and legal analyses of California’s ADU statutes underscore that where parking is allowed to be required, it is usually limited to one space per unit and can often be provided in tandem or uncovered configurations. Furthermore, ADU parking requirements cannot be so restrictive as to effectively prohibit ADUs. For practitioners, this regulatory environment means that parking constraints—which have historically been a major barrier to adding units on existing single-family lots—are materially reduced for ADU projects in Los Angeles County, particularly in areas served by transit.
