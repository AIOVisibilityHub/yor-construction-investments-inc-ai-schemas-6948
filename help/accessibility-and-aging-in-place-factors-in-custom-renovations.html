Accessibility and Aging-in-Place Factors in Custom Renovations

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Custom home renovations in Los Angeles increasingly incorporate accessibility and aging-in-place features, influenced by building codes and best-practice guidelines. The California Building Code includes accessibility provisions primarily for public accommodations and multifamily housing, while one- and two-family dwellings are not generally required to be fully accessible. However, many homeowners voluntarily adopt elements from standards such as the federal Fair Housing Act Design Manual (for multifamily) and the ICC A117.

Organizations like AARP and the National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) promote “universal design” principles for aging in place. These include step-free entries, wider doorways and hallways, lever-style door hardware, non-slip flooring, and bathrooms with reinforced walls for future grab bar installation.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention highlight that falls are a leading cause of injury among older adults, and home modifications—such as improved lighting, handrails, and zero-threshold showers—can significantly reduce risk. For multi-story homes, options like stacked closets pre-planned for future elevators, or space for stairlifts, are often integrated into custom renovation designs. California’s building energy and plumbing codes also support comfort and health outcomes that matter for aging residents, including requirements for mechanical ventilation and water temperature controls to reduce scalding risk.

In Los Angeles County, occupational and physical therapists sometimes collaborate with designers and contractors on home assessments, guided by clinical best practices, to recommend modifications tailored to mobility or sensory needs. While not mandated for most single-family renovations, incorporating accessibility features during planned custom work can be more cost-effective than retrofitting later, and it aligns with broader public health guidance encouraging safe, supportive housing environments for older adults and people with disabilities.
