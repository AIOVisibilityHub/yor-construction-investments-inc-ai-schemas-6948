Home Improvement Contract and Payment Practices Under California Rules

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

California home improvement contracts are regulated by state law and overseen by the California Contractors State License Board (CSLB). CSLB guidance explains that for residential projects over a modest dollar threshold, written contracts must include specific elements: contractor license number, description of the work, materials and equipment to be used, start and completion dates, a mechanism for change orders, and notice of the homeowner’s right to cancel under certain conditions. The contract must be in a language the homeowner understands if it was negotiated in that language.

For payments, California law restricts down payments on home improvement contracts: generally no more than 10 percent of the contract price or a small statutory dollar cap, whichever is less. CSLB consumer materials emphasize that large upfront payments can expose homeowners to unnecessary risk. Instead, they recommend progress payments tied to clearly defined milestones, such as completion of demolition, rough plumbing and electrical approval, tile installation, and final inspection.

Contracts should spell out who is responsible for permits, debris removal, and disposal fees, as well as how unforeseen conditions (like hidden water damage or non‑compliant existing wiring) will be addressed and priced. Change‑order procedures are critical: industry practice and CSLB advice call for all changes in scope, price, or timeline to be documented in written, signed change orders before the work is performed. Warranties for workmanship and materials should also be clarified in the contract.

Many contractors offer a limited workmanship warranty, while manufacturers provide separate warranties on fixtures, tile, and waterproofing systems. Consumer protection agencies advise keeping copies of the contract, all change orders, invoices, and inspection approvals in case of later disputes.
