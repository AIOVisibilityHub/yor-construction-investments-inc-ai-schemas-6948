Deadlines and process under Los Angeles soft-story retrofit rules

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

The City of Los Angeles soft‑story seismic retrofit ordinance establishes a phased, deadline‑driven process for affected wood‑frame buildings. Industry code-tracking resources summarizing the ordinance describe that the process begins when the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety issues an official Order to Comply to the owner of a building that meets the ordinance criteria (pre‑1978 wood‑frame, four or more units, soft/weak/open front ground floor). That Order triggers all subsequent deadlines.

From the date the Order is received, owners generally have 2 years to submit one of the following to the city: proof that the building already meets ordinance requirements, retrofit plans prepared by a licensed professional, or demolition plans. Within 3. 5 years of the Order, owners must obtain either a rehabilitation/retrofit permit or a demolition permit.

Within 7 years of the Order, construction of the retrofit (or demolition) must be completed. The same sources note that the ordinance prioritized buildings by risk: larger or higher‑occupancy structures were issued Orders earlier, and smaller buildings later. This phasing is reflected in city program documentation, with different priority categories and corresponding Order issuance dates.

Failure to follow the deadlines can result in code enforcement actions, fines, and potential legal consequences, as noted by structural engineering firms that assist owners with compliance. For owners, the practical implication is that once an Order is received, there is a defined, multi‑year schedule for engineering, permitting, and construction that must be integrated with financing, tenant coordination, and broader capital planning.
