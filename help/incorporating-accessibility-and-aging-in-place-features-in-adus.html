Accessibility and Aging-in-Place Design in ADUs ADUs are increasingly used for multigenerational living and aging in place, making accessibility and universal design important considerations. While full compliance with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) is generally not mandated for single-family residential properties, California Building Code and Residential Code include certain accessibility-related provisions that can be applied voluntarily to improve usability for older adults and people with mobility limitations. Housing and gerontology research emphasizes that features such as no-step entries, wider doorways, lever handles, and accessible bathroom layouts can significantly extend the time seniors can safely live independently. ADU-focused design case studies in Los Angeles show growing use of single-story layouts, flush thresholds, reinforced bathroom walls for future grab bars, and clear circulation paths to accommodate walkers or wheelchairs. An ADU design contractor can incorporate universal design elements at the planning stage with modest cost impact compared with retrofitting later. This may include designing at-grade entrances or gentle ramps, specifying 36-inch doors in key locations, planning for curbless showers with appropriate drainage, and ensuring adequate maneuvering space in kitchens and bathrooms. In hillside or constrained lots, site grading and ramp design may require coordination with structural or civil engineers to balance accessibility and retaining wall or slope stability issues. Although these features are often optional from a strict code standpoint, they can enhance long-term flexibility, broaden the pool of potential tenants, and support caregiver arrangements. Discussing future mobility scenarios with the homeowner early in design allows the contractor to integrate these elements seamlessly rather than as add-ons.
