Incentives and tax policies shaping solar economics in LA The federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC) is a central policy driver for solar economics in Los Angeles County. IRS guidance allows eligible taxpayers to claim a percentage of qualified residential or commercial solar project costs as a dollar‑for‑dollar credit against income tax liability. For businesses, federal tax policy also permits accelerated depreciation of solar assets, improving after‑tax returns. At the state level, California does not currently provide a broad, ongoing solar tax credit for residential systems, but it supports distributed energy through programs like the Self‑Generation Incentive Program (SGIP) for storage. Program rules from the California Public Utilities Commission describe incentives that vary by customer class and location, with higher levels for low‑income customers and those in high‑fire‑threat areas. These incentives directly reduce upfront battery costs and can be combined with the federal ITC when storage is paired with solar under qualifying conditions. Local utility incentives in Los Angeles have evolved over time. LADWP’s Solar Incentive Program historically offered per‑watt rebates that stepped down as installed capacity targets were met. Program documentation explains that remaining funds and incentive levels are periodically updated, and applications are processed on a first‑come, first‑served basis. Some municipal utilities in the county may offer specialized programs for low‑income customers or specific technologies. Property‑tax treatment is another factor. California’s property‑tax rules generally provide favorable treatment for active solar energy systems added to existing properties, limiting or excluding their value from property‑tax assessments under certain conditions. State Board of Equalization guidance outlines the scope and timelines for this exclusion. Together, these federal, state, and local policies materially shape payback periods and internal rates of return and are important inputs into financial modeling for any Los Angeles solar project.
