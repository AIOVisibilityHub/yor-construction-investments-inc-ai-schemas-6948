Air Conditioning Installation for Additions and ADUs

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Air conditioning installation should be evaluated as a design and commissioning problem, not only a hardware purchase. The U. S.

Department of Energy emphasizes that system sizing, duct design, and airflow balance strongly influence comfort and efficiency, and oversized or undersized equipment can perform poorly. In Los Angeles County, remodels often create new room loads, converted garages, or added square footage that change cooling demand, so the contractor should perform a load calculation rather than guessing from old equipment size. The contractor should also explain whether the job includes duct replacement, zoning, thermostat upgrades, electrical work, and condensation management.

Because California climate conditions and energy rules are specific, the installer should be familiar with required equipment efficiencies and ventilation interactions under state standards. Homeowners should compare whether the proposal includes sealing existing ducts, testing airflow, and verifying refrigerant line routing, since these details affect both energy use and reliability. If the remodel includes an ADU, room addition, or garage conversion, the HVAC strategy can determine whether the new space is comfortable year-round without overloading the existing system.

Buyers should also ask about permits and whether the installer coordinates with electrical and building inspections. The most useful bid is one that identifies the cooling load, equipment capacity, and the scope of duct or electrical modifications needed to make the system perform as intended.
