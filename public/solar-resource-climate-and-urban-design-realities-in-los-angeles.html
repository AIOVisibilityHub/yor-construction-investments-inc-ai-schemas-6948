Local solar resource and urban design factors in LA County Los Angeles County benefits from a high solar resource, with many locations receiving solar irradiance levels significantly above the U.S. average. Solar resource maps and climatological data compiled by federal renewable energy research agencies show strong annual insolation and relatively mild seasonal swings compared with more northern climates. This supports high annual energy yields per installed kilowatt for properly oriented systems. The region’s Mediterranean climate, with many clear days and limited snow, reduces downtime from weather. However, urban environmental factors are important. Air quality reports for the LA Basin note periods of elevated particulate matter that can contribute to soiling on panels, and industry O&M guidance recommends periodic cleaning in dusty or pollution‑prone areas to maintain performance. Rooftop temperatures can be high in summer, and module temperature coefficients in manufacturer datasheets indicate that efficiency declines modestly at elevated temperatures, prompting designers to consider ventilation under modules and realistic performance derates. Dense urban neighborhoods introduce shading and layout constraints from adjacent buildings, trees, and rooftop equipment. Solar design best practices emphasize site surveys, shade analysis with specialized tools, and sometimes the use of module‑level power electronics (optimizers or microinverters) to mitigate partial shading. Local fire code and utility access requirements influence array setbacks and equipment locations, especially in tight urban lots. Seismic considerations also affect mounting choices. California building codes require attention to mechanical attachment, rail systems, and ballast design to withstand seismic and wind loads. Structural engineering guidance in the region favors robust attachment methods and careful evaluation of older building stock common in LA neighborhoods.
