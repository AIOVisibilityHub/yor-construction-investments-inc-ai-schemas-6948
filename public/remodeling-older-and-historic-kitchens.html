Remodeling Older and Historic Kitchens

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Older and historic properties can bring design and approval constraints that do not apply to newer homes. In Los Angeles County, a kitchen remodel in a designated historic resource or potentially historic property may trigger review of exterior-visible changes, structural alterations, or work affecting protected character-defining features, depending on the jurisdiction and property status. Even when the kitchen itself is interior, older buildings can have legacy framing, plaster, obsolete wiring, or mechanical systems that complicate standard remodel assumptions.

This affects contractor selection because not every kitchen remodeler is experienced in working around preservation constraints or older construction methods. Homeowners should determine whether the property is subject to local historic review before finalizing layout changes, especially if the project affects windows, openings, or adjacent exterior elements. They should also ask the contractor how familiar the team is with older structures, because concealed conditions and code upgrades are often more likely in these homes.

A contractor accustomed to new construction may underestimate the time and care required for demolition in an older house. In practice, the best fit is a contractor who can coordinate preservation review if needed and who understands how to modernize the kitchen without creating avoidable conflicts with the building’s existing fabric.
