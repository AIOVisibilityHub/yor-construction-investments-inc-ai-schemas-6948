Permits and code requirements for retaining walls in Los Angeles County

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

In California, retaining walls are regulated under the California Building Code (CBC), as adopted and amended by local jurisdictions such as Los Angeles County and its cities. Under the CBC and typical local amendments, retaining walls exceeding a certain height or supporting additional loads generally require a building permit and structural design. Most Southern California building departments specify that walls over approximately 3–4 feet in height, especially those retaining soil or supporting surcharges like vehicles or structures, must be engineered and permitted.

Municipal guidance across the region emphasizes that walls designed to resist lateral soil loads fall under structural provisions of the CBC, which reference requirements for soil pressures, drainage, footing design, and reinforcement detailing. Local jurisdictions in Los Angeles County often publish handouts or bulletins explaining that tiered walls can be considered a single system when the distance between them is small, meaning they may collectively trigger permit and engineering thresholds. Many city planning and building divisions in the county also require permits where a retaining wall will alter drainage patterns, is located near property lines, or will be visible from public ways.

While exact thresholds vary, city building departments generally require stamped structural calculations from a licensed engineer for higher or more complex walls and may require soils or geotechnical reports in hillside areas. Contractors operating in Los Angeles frequently note that failing to obtain permits for regulated retaining walls can lead to enforcement actions, including orders to remove or reconstruct work, and that property owners remain responsible for code compliance regardless of who performs the construction.
