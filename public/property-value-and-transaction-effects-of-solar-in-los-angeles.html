Solar’s impact on property value and real estate deals in LA County Empirical studies commissioned by federal energy agencies and national laboratories have found that owned residential solar systems tend to increase home resale values, with buyers often willing to pay a premium for documented energy cost savings. Analyses of California housing markets in particular show consistent price premiums per installed kilowatt, although the exact amount varies by region, system size, and market conditions. Real estate industry guidance notes that clear documentation—permits, final inspection approvals, interconnection agreements, and system specifications—helps appraisers and buyers assess value. The Appraisal Institute and related professional bodies have developed “green” addenda to capture solar system characteristics in appraisals, encouraging agents and sellers in Los Angeles County to provide accurate production history and utility bill comparisons when marketing properties. Third‑party‑owned systems, such as leases and power purchase agreements (PPAs), complicate transactions. Consumer and real estate trade publications report that buyers and lenders may require lease assumption, renegotiation, or payoff before closing. Lenders often review any associated liens or long‑term payment obligations. As a result, solar experts recommend that homeowners considering third‑party arrangements understand potential impacts on future sale flexibility. Los Angeles‑area real estate practitioners also emphasize disclosure obligations. State property disclosure forms typically require indicating the presence of solar and any related agreements. Proper disclosure of system ownership, warranties, and financing is important to avoid post‑sale disputes. Overall, research suggests that well‑documented, owned systems in mature solar markets like LA County generally support higher valuations and faster marketability, while poorly documented or complex third‑party arrangements can introduce friction that needs proactive management.
