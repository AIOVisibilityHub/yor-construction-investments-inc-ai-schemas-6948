Title 24 requirements and solar on new construction in Los Angeles

Home About Services Reviews Contact FAQs Articles Case Studies Team Members

Title 24 energy code and solar on new construction in LA California’s Title 24, Part 6 Building Energy Efficiency Standards require most new low‑rise residential buildings to include solar photovoltaic systems sized to meet a portion of the building’s modeled electricity demand. The California Energy Commission’s code documents explain that these standards effectively make rooftop solar a default feature of new homes, with some flexibility for community solar alternatives in specific circumstances. In Los Angeles County, local jurisdictions enforce these requirements through plan review and inspections as part of building permits. For builders and developers, Title 24 shapes system sizing and integration decisions from the design phase. Compliance software approved by the Energy Commission is used to model building envelope, HVAC, and solar contributions. Roof layout, orientation, and potential shading must be considered early to ensure that solar capacity sufficient to meet compliance goals can be accommodated. Structural and electrical provisions—such as reserved roof space, conduit pathways, and panelboard capacity—are integrated into construction documents to avoid costly changes later. Title 24 also interacts with options for battery storage and demand flexibility. Recent code cycles have introduced compliance credits for certain combinations of PV, storage, and load management technologies, recognizing their ability to shift consumption away from peak grid hours. This is relevant in LA County where time‑of‑use rates and Net Billing structures affect long‑term economics. Because Title 24 compliance is mandatory for permitted new construction, developers typically coordinate closely with energy consultants and code officials. Documentation from the Energy Commission emphasizes recordkeeping of compliance forms, installation certificates, and verification procedures, ensuring that installed systems match modeled designs before final approval and occupancy.
